The first asks whether the model uses the parts it points to. We rank the six body parts by saliency, mask them in that order, and compare against the reverse. The important parts cost more: a gap of 0.124, positive in every fold, 0.199 at 25-joint resolution.

The model reads the body the way a movement analyst would

Laban Movement Analysis is a movement analyst's vocabulary for how a body moves. People wrote its rules; nothing learned them from our recordings.

  • Saliency over the four regions, head, arms, legs and torso, ranks the way the Laban attributes do: ρ = +0.500.
  • Classical kinematics is the control that makes +0.500 a result: per-joint speed, acceleration, range of motion and energy reach +0.033, about 15 times weaker.
  • Sadness reads as a bowed, sunken posture: head down, trunk forward.

One strong member. The 12 emotions are not 12 independent observations, so the 95% CI [+0.150, +0.733] is an emotion-block bootstrap and the null permutes regions within an emotion, p < 0.001. Positive for 10 of the 12 emotions (sign test p = 0.039, median ρ = +0.70).

On its own that would just be a number, so we chose a control. Classical kinematics through the identical pipeline reaches 0.033, about fifteen times weaker. The contrast is the finding.

The statistics respect that structure: the interval resamples emotions and the null shuffles regions inside an emotion. Ten of the twelve are positive.

In plain language: for sadness the head bows, the body is carried lower, the trunk leans forward.

The negative we report

Inside the half-second of movement the model reads, every frame counts about the same. Across the body, the head and neck dominate.

The window is 64 frames, about half a second at 120 fps. Across frames the important-versus-reverse gap is about +0.002, against +0.124 ± 0.031 across body parts, and frame saliency entropy is 98.7% of its maximum per clip, 99.5% as a class mean. Order still matters: time reversal moves accuracy, so this is a claim about locating a frame.

This is the negative, and I think it is what makes the rest of this a test. Every model here reads a 64-frame window, about half a second. Ask which frames inside it matter and the answer is all of them, roughly equally.

Frame saliency entropy is 98.7 percent of maximum, and masking frames in importance order costs almost nothing more than the reverse, where the same measurement over body parts gave a clear positive. The evidence is spread through time and concentrated in space.

To be precise, this is about locating a single frame, not time in general. Order still matters; time reversal moves accuracy. We report the null rather than hiding it.
