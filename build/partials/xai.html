This is the main result. Laban Movement Analysis is the vocabulary movement professionals use for how a body moves: how far the head bows, how far the trunk leans, how high the body is carried. People wrote those rules down, and nothing in them learned from our recordings.

So we have two rankings of the four regions, head, arms, legs and torso. One is how much saliency the model puts on each region, the other is what the Laban attributes say for that emotion, and they agree at 0.500. The control is classical kinematics, per-joint speed, acceleration, range of motion and energy, through the identical pipeline, and it reaches 0.033, about fifteen times weaker.

The statistics respect the structure here, because the forty-eight pairs are twelve emotions of four regions. The interval resamples emotions and the null shuffles regions inside an emotion. Ten of the twelve come out positive.

What I like about it is that it reads in plain language. For sadness the model uses what you would expect: the head bows, the body is carried lower, the trunk leans forward.

The negative we report

Inside the half-second of movement the model reads, every frame counts about the same. Across the body, the head and neck dominate.

The window is 64 frames, about half a second at 120 fps. Across frames the important-versus-reverse gap is about +0.002, against +0.124 ± 0.031 across body parts, and frame saliency entropy is 98.7% of its maximum per clip, 99.5% as a class mean. Order still matters: time reversal moves accuracy, so this is a claim about locating a frame.

This is the negative, and I think it is what makes the rest of this a test. Every model here reads a 64-frame window, about half a second of movement. Ask which frames inside it matter and the answer is all of them, roughly equally.

Frame saliency entropy is 98.7 percent of maximum, and masking frames in importance order costs almost nothing more than the reverse order, where the same measurement over body parts gave a clear positive. On the right is the other side of it: the head and neck dominate in all twelve emotions. The evidence is spread through time and concentrated in space.

To be precise, this is about locating a single frame, not about time in general. Order still matters, and time reversal moves accuracy. We report the null rather than hiding it.

If someone asks about the fall at the ends of the curve: saliency tapers over roughly the last eight frames and the first one, from about 0.018 to 0.004 against a flat 0.0156. The window edges fall away, no interior frame does, and the entropy stays at 99.5 percent. That is why the claim rests on the entropy and the gap rather than on the shape of the curve.
