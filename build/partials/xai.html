The negative we report

Inside the half-second of movement the model reads, every frame counts about the same. Across the body, the head and neck dominate.

The window is 64 frames, about half a second at 120 fps. Across frames the important-versus-reverse gap is about +0.002, against +0.124 ± 0.031 across body parts, and frame saliency entropy is 98.7% of its maximum per clip, 99.5% as a class mean. Order still matters: time reversal moves accuracy, so this is a claim about locating a frame.

Here's the negative, and it's what tells you the other five could have failed. Every model here reads a 64-frame window, about half a second. If you ask which frames inside that window matter, the answer is all of them, more or less equally.

Frame saliency entropy is 98.7 percent of maximum, and masking frames in importance order costs almost nothing more than masking them in reverse. The same measurement over body parts gave a clear positive.

This is a claim about locating a single frame, not about time in general. Order still matters: reverse the clip and accuracy moves. We report the null rather than hiding it.
